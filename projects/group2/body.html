Gut genomes shifted after a diet switch while the community's carbohydrate-degradation potential stayed nearly constant

Abstract

Diet can change the human gut microbiome within days, but daily, genome-resolved views of how single genomes and the community's carbohydrate-degrading genes respond are rare. We followed one person over ten daily stool samples, five days on a high-carbohydrate, high-calorie diet and five on a low-carbohydrate, low-calorie diet, using metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) from nanopore long reads. Community composition differed between the periods (Bray-Curtis dissimilarity between days of different periods, and within them), but the largest change came one to two days after the switch, and diversity did not change. Of MAGs with a mean share of at least 0.1%, changed at least two-fold ( down, all Bacillota; up); for of them the 90% day-resampling interval of the change (descriptive) excluded zero, and only changed most at the switch itself. The community's degrading CAZyme genes per 1,000 genes changed by % (day-resampling interval to %), with a larger rise for host-glycan genes (%); Bacteroidota MAGs replacing each other accounted for most of the change. Because diet is confounded with time in one person, these are descriptions, not tests. They suggest that the community's membership changed between the periods more than its overall carbohydrate potential.

Introduction

The gut microbiome responds fast to diet: in a controlled study, switching people to an entirely plant-based or animal-based diet changed community structure within days, and the animal-based diet lowered Firmicutes that break down plant polysaccharides (Roseburia, Eubacterium rectale) [1]. With daily sampling, community composition depended on several days of dietary history, and the responses were personal [2]. Lowering carbohydrate intake reduced the Roseburia / E. rectale group and faecal butyrate in a weight-loss study [3].

Which carbohydrates a community can use is written in its carbohydrate-active enzyme (CAZyme) genes. Gut bacteria carry a large and varied CAZyme repertoire [4], and Bacteroidota organise much of theirs in polysaccharide utilisation loci, gene clusters each dedicated, usually, to one type of glycan [5]. When dietary fibre is scarce, gut bacteria can turn to host mucus glycans, as shown in gnotobiotic mice colonised with a synthetic human gut community [6], so a change in diet could shift the balance between plant-fibre and host-glycan enzymes. Sequencing measures shares, not amounts: a rise of one group pushes all others down, so relative abundance has to be read as a composition [7].

Long-read metagenomics recovers many near-complete genomes from one sample series [8], which allows following single genomes and their gene content day by day. Here we use the course's own nanopore data set: one person, ten consecutive daily stool samples, days 1-5 on a high-carbohydrate, high-calorie diet and days 6-10 on a low-carbohydrate, low-calorie diet. Both periods were vegetarian; the days 6-10 diet was closer to the person's normal diet, and the person had eaten a similar high-calorie diet for about a week before day 1, so there is no sample from before it (information from the data owner).

We asked two questions, each with the group's hypothesis. Research question 1 (community dynamics): how do composition, diversity and single MAGs change over time and between the diet periods, and is a MAG's shift a step at the switch or a gradual trend? Hypothesis 1: community structure changes with the diet transition, with specific MAGs consistently rising or falling. Research question 2 (functional dynamics): how does the community's carbohydrate-degradation potential change between the periods, and which MAGs contribute most? Hypothesis 2: carbohydrate utilisation potential differs between the periods. Expected results were written down before each analysis (Methods and the rationale document).

Discussion

Hypothesis 1 holds in a narrow sense: community structure differed between the periods, and specific MAGs rose or fell consistently, robust to day resampling, leaving out days, other cut-offs and a compositional transform. The change came one to two days after the switch rather than at it, for the community as a whole and for most single MAGs. One explanation is that composition depends on several days of diet history [2], consistent with diet changing community structure within days [1]. Bacteroidota is the exception that jumps at the switch and then falls; the two phases may be different responses (for example to the change in food, then to the lower calories), but ten days cannot tell. The fallers are the butyrate producers that earlier work saw drop with less carbohydrate [3], consistent with a diet effect, but diet is confounded with time and order.

Hypothesis 2 is not supported in any large way: total CAZyme potential changed by a few percent, with an interval that includes zero, and no common family changed 1.5-fold. Host-glycan genes rose and starch and fructan genes fell, with intervals on one side of zero, but these are a few genes per 1,000 and were among several groups examined. One reading is functional redundancy: MAGs with similar repertoires replaced each other, so the community kept its toolkit while its membership changed (our interpretation). The small shifts trace to Bacteroidota, whose utilisation loci make them major carriers of CAZymes [4, 5]. Gene content does not say how much each enzyme is used; in [1] diet changed microbial gene expression, which a DNA-only study cannot see.

Limitations. One person and one switch: days are not replicates, so nothing here is a test, and diet is confounded with time, order and the preceding week of the same high-calorie diet. Shares of DNA are not cell counts, and only the binned -% of bases are counted. Sequencing depth was higher in days 6-10; well-covered MAGs show the same pattern, but within days 1-5 deeper samples sat further along axis 1 (Spearman on five days), which we cannot explain. The plant-fibre result depends on two medium-quality P. copri_B MAGs. CAZyme calls come from one HMM search, substrate groups are broad, and incomplete MAGs miss genes (completeness and CAZyme genes per 1,000 genes were unrelated, Spearman ).

Unresolved questions. Does the community return towards a usual state? Samples from a normal week (not measured) would show whether days 8-10 approach them. Are the two P. copri_B MAGs one genome split in two or two strains (their variant and coverage data could tell)? Did butyrate potential fall with the Bacillota (the same weighting on butyrate pathway genes)? Which glycans do the rising and falling Bacteroidota specialise in (grouping their CAZymes into utilisation loci [5])? Does the unbinned fifth of the bases change the same way (a read-based profile)? Is the depth link within days 1-5 chance or a technical effect (a re-run of the shallow samples)?

Methods

Study design and samples. One adult gave a stool sample on ten consecutive days; days 1-5 were high carbohydrate and high calorie, days 6-10 low carbohydrate and low calorie, both vegetarian (course sample sheet; diet details from the data owner as stated in the Introduction). No food records, no sample before day 1, no replicate extractions and no RNA, protein, metabolite or cell-count data are available (Supplementary Table 1 lists the inputs used).

Sequencing, assembly and binning. DNA was sequenced on one PromethION flow cell (R10.4.1, Rapid Barcoding Kit) and basecalled with Dorado (super-accurate model; module 8). Reads were filtered to Q ≥ 10 and ≥ 1,000 bp (chopper 0.14.1) and adapters trimmed (Porechop_ABI 0.5.1); per-sample yields are in Supplementary Table 2. The ten samples were co-assembled and binned with mmlong2 1.2.1 [8] (myloasm assembler), giving MAGs. Completeness and contamination come from CheckM2 [9]; mmlong2 classes MAGs as high quality (HQ; ≥ 90% complete, ≤ 5% contamination, rRNA and tRNA genes present, after MIMAG [10]) or medium quality (MQ; ≥ 50%, < 10%): HQ and MQ (module 5). Taxonomy is from GTDB-Tk 2.5.2 [11].

Relative abundance and normalisation. A MAG's share on a day is the percent of that day's sequenced bases that map to it (module 4), so it is independent of the total depth by construction. Phylum shares are sums of MAG shares. For Bray-Curtis and diversity, each day's MAG shares are rescaled to sum 1 (shares of the binned bases).

Composition, ordination and diversity. Bray-Curtis dissimilarity between days uses all MAGs; principal coordinates analysis (classical scaling) maps the days so that distances follow it, and all positive axes together rebuild the matrix exactly. As a compositional alternative, centred log-ratios (natural log of share + 0.01%, minus each day's mean over the MAGs) give the Aitchison distance (Euclidean distance on them) [7]. Diversity is the Shannon index on the MAGs with a mean share of at least 0.1%, shown as its effective number of MAGs.

MAG trajectories, step and trend. Period change = log2 of the mean share in days 6-10 over days 1-5, with 0.01% added to both. On log2(share + 0.01%) per day, each of the nine splits of the ten days into an early and a late block is fitted by two block means; its R² is compared with that of a straight line over the days (both two parameters, so neither is favoured by size). The split with the highest R² is the best split; a best split at 5|6 fits a step at the switch, a later one a delayed step, and a line that beats the 5|6 step fits a gradual trend. Separation counts the low-carbohydrate days beyond all five high-carbohydrate days. The same split check is applied to PCoA axis 1.

Uncertainty and sensitivity. With one person and one sample per day there are no replicate samples, so every effect size gets two descriptive checks of how much it depends on the particular days that were sampled. Day resampling: for each period we drew five of its own five days at random with replacement (a day can be drawn twice and another not at all), averaged each MAG's share over the drawn days, and recomputed the log2 change of the two period means with the same 0.01% pseudocount. Repeated times (fixed seed), this gives log2 changes per MAG; their 5th and 95th percentiles are the 90% day-resampling interval (Fig. 4a). The same drawn days were used for every contrast (CAZyme potential, phyla, diversity). A worked example with one MAG's numbers is in the rationale document (section 3.7). This interval is descriptive and too narrow: even for independent days, the spread of a resampled five-day mean is about √(4/5) of the true standard error; each period has only 126 distinct resamples; and neighbouring days are autocorrelated. Intervals for MAGs selected for a large change will exclude zero almost by construction. Leave one day out: each of the ten days is removed once and all contrasts recomputed on the other nine (ten runs); a MAG counts as two-fold in all runs if its change stays at least two-fold, and its mean share over the nine days stays at or above 0.1%, in every run ( fail only the share cut-off). Cut-off grid: fold-change cut-offs 1.5, 2, 3 and 4; mean-share cut-offs 0.05, 0.1, 0.25 and 0.5%; pseudocounts 0.001, 0.01 and 0.1% ( settings). Quality: all analyses repeated on HQ MAGs only. Depth: bases per sample against PCoA axis 1 and the binned share (Spearman), and Bray-Curtis on the MAGs with a mean share of at least 0.5% only.

CAZyme annotation and community potential. Genes were predicted with Pyrodigal 3.7.1 [12] and searched against the dbCAN HMMs (release db_v5-2-9_5-5-2026) with pyhmmer 0.12.3 [13]; degrading families (glycoside hydrolases, polysaccharide lyases, carbohydrate esterases) are grouped by substrate following dbCAN3 [14] (module 7). Per day, the community value is Σ share × CAZyme genes / Σ share × all genes × 1,000 over all MAGs; a period value is the mean of its five days. Per 1,000 genes rather than per genome, the value does not grow with genome size or completeness.

Contribution decomposition. A MAG's contribution to the change is 1,000 × (its change in mean share) × (its genes in the group − the community's days 1-5 rate × its genes) / (the community's share-weighted gene count in days 6-10). The contributions add up exactly to the change computed at the period-mean shares; phylum values are sums over their MAGs. A MAG pushes the value up if it rises and is richer than the community in that group, or falls and is poorer.

Statistics and reporting. No hypothesis tests and no p-values: the ten days of one person are a time series, not replicates, so no multiple-testing correction is needed or applied; instead we report effect sizes with resampling intervals and sensitivity ranges, and the number of contrasts looked at (rationale document). Positive controls: recomputed phylum shares, Bray-Curtis values and two-fold counts equal the course's earlier tables; weighting a single MAG at 100% returns its own CAZyme rate (Bacteroides cellulosilyticus: genes per 1,000 genes, as in module 7); CAZyme counts per MAG and family equal module 7's own gene search; resampling with the original days, leaving no day out and the default cut-off setting each return the main numbers; the analysis script stops if any control fails.
