Community and carbohydrate potential over the ten days

A first answer to group 2's question, worked out on the course data. Every number on this page is computed from the data by the course's analysis script; hover over a chart to read its values, and open the guided examples to check a result yourself.

The question

The group asked two things. First: how do the community's composition, its diversity and single MAGs change over the ten days and between the two diet periods (days 1-5 high carbohydrate and high calorie, days 6-10 low carbohydrate and low calorie), and is a MAG's shift a step at the diet switch or a gradual trend over the days? Second: does the community's potential to break down carbohydrates differ between the periods, and which MAGs cause the difference?

We answer both as descriptions of one person over ten days. The diet changed once, at the same time for everything, so diet and time cannot be told apart, and there are no repeats of the switch to test against. Both periods were vegetarian, and the days 6-10 diet was closer to the person's normal diet; the subject had eaten a similar high-calorie diet for about a week before day 1, so days 1-5 continue that diet and the switch at day 6 is the main change in the study, with no sample taken before the high-calorie diet began (information from the data owner). A gene in a genome is potential, not activity: this study measured DNA, not which genes were used. The group asked for a full manuscript; this page gives the analyses, short methods and the limits instead.

Guided examples

Data used

All from our own sequencing run of the ten samples (module 8, Project data). The MAG abundance table has one row per MAG and one column per day: the percent of that day's sequenced bases that map to the MAG (module 4). It covers MAGs, all high or medium quality (module 5); together they hold -% of each day's bases, the rest is not in a MAG. The taxonomy of each MAG (GTDB) gives the phylum.

For the second question we use the genes of every MAG and their CAZyme calls (carbohydrate-active enzymes; the same gene search and substrate groups as module 7): per MAG, the number of degrading CAZyme genes (glycoside hydrolases, polysaccharide lyases and carbohydrate esterases) in each substrate group, and the MAG's total number of genes.

Approach

Composition. Phylum shares per day are the summed shares of the MAGs in each phylum. How different two days are is measured by the Bray-Curtis dissimilarity on all MAG shares (0 = the same MAGs in the same proportions, 1 = nothing shared); a principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) draws the ten days as points so that distances on the map follow Bray-Curtis. Diversity is the Shannon index on the MAGs with a mean share of at least 0.1% over the ten days, shown as its effective number of MAGs (the number of equally common MAGs that would give the same index).

Single MAGs. The same MAGs. The period change is log2 of the mean share in days 6-10 over the mean in days 1-5 (0.01% added to both, so absent MAGs stay finite); 1 is a doubling. For the break check we split the ten days at each of the nine places (day 1 | days 2-10, ..., days 1-9 | day 10) and ask how much of the MAG's day-to-day variation (on a log scale) two block means explain (R², 0 to 1); the split with the highest R² is the best split. A straight line over the ten days (a trend) is fitted the same way. Separation counts how many of the five low-carbohydrate days lie above all five high-carbohydrate days (for a MAG that rises) or below them (for one that falls).

CAZyme potential. For each day: CAZyme genes per 1,000 genes of the binned community, each MAG weighted by its share of that day's bases (Σ share × CAZyme genes / Σ share × all genes × 1,000). What a MAG adds to the change between the periods is (its change in share) × (its CAZyme genes − the community's rate in days 1-5 × its genes), divided by the community's weighted gene count in days 6-10, times 1,000: a MAG pushes the value up if it gains share and carries more of these genes per gene than the community, or loses share and carries fewer. These contributions add up exactly to the change computed from the period-mean shares.

Expected and checked. Written before the run: days 1-6 alike, change from day 7; many MAG shifts later than the switch; CAZyme genes a few tens per 1,000 genes, the period difference below about 20%. Positive controls, all passed: the recomputed phylum shares, Bray-Curtis values and the counts of two-fold MAGs equal the course's earlier tables; with one MAG at 100% the weighting gives that MAG's own rate (Bacteroides cellulosilyticus: CAZyme genes per 1,000 genes, as in module 7).

What this does and does not show

Composition. The community changed between the periods, but not at the switch: day 6 is as close to days 1-5 ( on average) as those days are to each other (at most ), and days 8-10 are the most different (-). Diversity did not change (Shannon and ). Of the MAGs, fell and rose at least two-fold; all that fell are Bacillota.

Step or trend. of the two-fold MAGs change most after the switch, at it and before it. Compared without choosing the split, a step at the switch describes of them better than a straight line, a line . are completely separated (all five low-carbohydrate days beyond all five high-carbohydrate days). With ten days and one switch, a change one or two days late cannot be told from a trend that started anyway; the best split is chosen from nine, so it fits better by construction.

CAZyme potential. The community's CAZyme genes per 1,000 genes hardly differ between the periods (% for all groups together; starch %, plant fibre %, host glycan %). The changes come mostly from Bacteroidota trading places (two Prevotella copri_B MAGs down, Phocaeicola and Bacteroides up), not from the Bacillota that fell. This is potential: genes in genomes, weighted by DNA shares of the binned part of the community (about four fifths of the bases), not enzyme activity or cell numbers.

Next steps and questions for tomorrow

  • A return towards the usual diet. Days 6-10 were closer to the person's normal diet, after about two weeks of the high-calorie diet (one week before day 1 plus days 1-5; information from the data owner). Is the late change a community moving back towards its usual state? Without a sample from before the high-calorie diet this cannot be checked here; a sample from that time, or from a later normal week, would show it.
  • CAZyme families. The substrate groups pool many families. The same weighting on single families (module 8's CAZyme table per family) could show whether, for example, starch-degrading GH13 or host-glycan GH33 families move more than their group.
  • The two Prevotella copri_B MAGs drive much of the CAZyme change and keep a fixed ratio over the days: one genome split in two, or two strains? Their variant and coverage data (module 8) could tell.
  • The unbinned fifth. About a fifth of the bases is not in any MAG; a read-based profile (for example, a taxonomic classifier on the reads) would show whether it changes the same way. Days 11 onwards or a second switch would be needed to separate a diet response from time.
