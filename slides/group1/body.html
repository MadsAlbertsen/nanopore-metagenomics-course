How fast does the gut community follow a diet switch?

Ten daily stool samples from one person, sequenced with Oxford Nanopore long reads

Group 1 · Claude A. Gent et al. (2026)

This is group 1, and we asked how fast the gut community follows a diet switch. The data are ten daily stool samples from one person, sequenced with Oxford Nanopore long reads. Our answer, in short: in this person the community moved with a delay of one to two days.

[1] David et al. 2014, Nature 505:559. [2] Johnson et al. 2019, Cell Host Microbe 25:789. [3] Roager et al. 2016, Nat Microbiol 1:16093. [4] Asnicar et al. 2021, Gut 70:1665.

We cannot tell in advance when a diet change should show up in stool, because the delay can come from transit time, from the community, or from both. The diet switched between day 5 and day 6, and a similar high-calorie diet ran about a week before day 1; this comes from the data owner. Our question is whether the community changed on day 6 or later.

Result 2

Day 6 still old; the shift began on day 7

On day 6 the community was as close to day 1 as the early days were. It moved away from day 1 and towards the late days on days 7 and 8, a delay of one to two days. Days 9 and 10 showed no further clear trend.

Limitations

What the data cannot show

One person, one diet switch
Diet, time and calories changed together
No samples before day 1 or after day 10
Gene potential, not enzyme activity
Timing described, not tested; transit time not measured

These are the limits of what we can say. It is one person and one diet switch, and diet, time and calories changed together, so no cause can be named. We have no samples before day 1 or after day 10, the timing is described and not tested, and the enzyme result is gene potential, not activity.

Conclusions

Working with AI

Pros

  • AI has great potential to assist researchers for coding

Cons

  • Needs ample guidance and checks to ensure proper analyses and interpretation are completed
