How fast does the gut community follow a diet switch?

Ten daily stool samples from one person, sequenced with Oxford Nanopore long reads

Group 1 · Claude A. Gent et al. (2026)

The question in one line. The answer in one line: in this person the community moved with a delay of one to two days.

Result 1

Two blocks of similar days

Days 1-6 resemble each other, and so do days 8-10. Day 6, the first day of the new diet, still sat with the early days; the change lies between day 6 and day 8.

Bray-Curtis dissimilarity of every pair of days (brighter = more different). One person, n = 10 samples.

The split at the switch ranks second of possible splits (permutation p = ); neighbouring days are not independent, so this describes, it does not prove.

Result 2

Day 6 still old; the shift began on day 7

On day 6 the community was as close to day 1 as the early days were. It moved away from day 1 and towards the late days on days 7 and 8, a delay of one to two days. Days 9 and 10 showed no further clear trend.

Bray-Curtis dissimilarity of each day to day 1 and to the mean of days 8-10. One person, n = 10 samples.

A lag of one to two days fits transit time, but transit time was not measured.

Limitations

What the data cannot show

One person, one diet switch
Diet, time and calories changed together
No samples before day 1 or after day 10
Gene potential, not enzyme activity
Timing described, not tested; transit time not measured

No cause can be named: one person, a similar diet a week before day 1, diet mixed up with time and calories.

Conclusions

Conclusions

In this person the community followed the diet switch with a delay of one to two days.

Day 6 still looked like the old diet; the shift came on days 7 and 8
Enzyme-gene potential stayed near and per 1,000 genes (days 1-5, 6-10) while members replaced each other
One person, ten days: a description, not a cause
Our feelings about working with Claude

Working with AI

Pros

  • AI has great potential to assist researchers for coding
  • Rapid proof of concept testing, with subsequent refinement and testing
  • Highly efficient for generating figures, tables, data organization, etc.

Cons

  • Needs ample guidance and checks to ensure proper analyses and interpretation are completed
  • Wording often doesn't make sense (either hallucinations or grammatically incorrect)
  • Risk of trusting the interpretation and/or assumptions AI makes

There is a balance between working efficiently while including critical review and checks along the way.
