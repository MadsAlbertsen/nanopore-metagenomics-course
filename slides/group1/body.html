How fast does the gut community follow a diet switch?

Ten daily stool samples from one person, sequenced with Oxford Nanopore long reads

Group 1 · author names are not published

The question in one line. The answer in one line: in this person the community moved with a delay of one to two days.

Background

Why the timing is not obvious

  • In feeding studies the gut community changed within days of a new diet [1, 2]
  • Gut transit time differs between people, so a delay can come from transit, from the community, or both [3, 4]
  • Information from the data owner: days 1-5 high-carbohydrate, high-calorie; days 6-10 low-carbohydrate, low-calorie (both vegetarian); a similar high-calorie diet ran about a week before day 1
  • Our question: does the community change on day 6, the first day of the new diet, or later?

[1] David et al. 2014, Nature 505:559. [2] Johnson et al. 2019, Cell Host Microbe 25:789. [3] Roager et al. 2016, Nat Microbiol 1:16093. [4] Asnicar et al. 2021, Gut 70:1665.

Diet facts come from the data owner, not from our measurements. Carbohydrate and calories changed together.

Result 1

Two blocks of similar days

Days 1-6 resemble each other, and so do days 8-10. Day 6, the first day of the new diet, still sat with the early days; the change lies between day 6 and day 8.

Bray-Curtis dissimilarity of every pair of days (brighter = more different). One person, n = 10 samples.

The split at the switch ranks second of possible splits (permutation p = ); neighbouring days are not independent, so this describes, it does not prove.

Result 2

Day 6 still old; the shift began on day 7

On day 6 the community was as close to day 1 as the early days were. It moved away from day 1 and towards the late days on days 7 and 8, a delay of one to two days. Days 9 and 10 showed no further clear trend.

Bray-Curtis dissimilarity of each day to day 1 and to the mean of days 8-10. One person, n = 10 samples.

A lag of one to two days fits transit time, but transit time was not measured.

Result 3

Enzyme potential stayed level

Carbohydrate-degrading enzyme genes of the community stayed between and per 1,000 genes on every day, although genomes changed: genomes with large and small enzyme sets replaced each other.

Degrading CAZyme genes per 1,000 genes of the community (each genome weighted by its DNA share times its number of genes). Gene potential, not activity.

Rising Bacteroidaceae carry many such genes and falling Lachnospiraceae fewer; the sum stays level. Host-glycan genes did not track the Bacteroidaceae share.

Limitations

What the data cannot show

  • One person, one diet switch, one sample per day: the timing is described, not tested
  • Diet is mixed up with time and with calories; a similar diet ran a week before day 1, so no cause can be named
  • Relative abundance: a rise of one genome lowers the others; not cell numbers
  • No sample before day 1 or after day 10; transit time was not measured
  • Gene content is potential, not enzyme activity

Take-home

Take-home message

In this person the community followed the diet switch with a delay of one to two days.

  • Day 6 still looked like the old diet; the shift came on days 7 and 8
  • Enzyme-gene potential stayed near and genes per 1,000 genes (days 1-5 and 6-10) while members replaced each other
  • One person, ten days, diet mixed up with time and calories: a description, not a cause
