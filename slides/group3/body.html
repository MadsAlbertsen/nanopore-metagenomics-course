Butyrate gene potential is maintained but redistributed after a carbohydrate reduction: a ten-day long-read metagenomic time series

Group 3 · author names are not published

Title is the title of our article. One person, ten daily stool samples, long-read sequencing.

The question and why it matters

Does a person's butyrate gene potential change within days of eating fewer carbohydrates?

  • Butyrate is the main energy source of the cells lining the colon
  • Gut bacteria make it in a last step, either with a CoA-transferase (but) or with a kinase route (ptb + buk)
  • Question: after a carbohydrate reduction, does the gene potential for the two last steps change, and in which genomes?

Simplified from Figure 4 of our article, without data. Dashed: not searched (upstream genes and the amino-acid routes).

The pathway is the acetyl-CoA route to butyryl-CoA, then two ways to release butyrate. The CoA-transferase uses acetate; the kinase route makes ATP. We searched bcd and the final-step genes, not thl, hbd, crt or the lysine, glutarate and 4-aminobutyrate routes.

Data and approach

What we did with the samples

daily stool samples, one person
genomes (MAGs), of high quality
butyrate gene calls checked
  • Days 1-5 high-carbohydrate, high-calorie and days 6-10 low-carbohydrate, low-calorie, both vegetarian (information from the data owner)
  • Nanopore long reads, assembled and binned into MAGs; genes found with KOfam profiles, but by its Bakta name
  • Gene abundance: copies per 1,000 genes, each MAG weighted by its share of the DNA times its number of genes
  • Checks: DNA outside the MAGs, doubtful gene calls, ratios within each sample, dilution

Gene abundance is gene potential, not activity, and relative, not cell counts. The analysis is descriptive: one person, ten days.

Result 3: do the genes change?

Gene but falls, ptb and buk rise, bcd stays level

Left: change, days 1-5 to 6-10. Right (Fig 6): log2 of each day's gene copies per 1,000 genes vs the mean of days 1-5; columns are days. but to , ptb to , buk to copies per 1,000 genes. Gene potential, not activity.

The fall of but is carried mostly by Agathobacter and Roseburia; the rise of ptb and buk by Acetatifactor, with Butyrivibrio (ptb) and Phocaeicola (buk). Phocaeicola has no ptb, so its buk is probably not part of a butyrate route. The ratio but / (ptb + buk) fell by log2 .

Limitations

What the data cannot show

  • One person, one sample per day, ten days: descriptive, not tested
  • Diet is confounded with time; carbohydrate and calories changed together; protein and fat were not recorded
  • Days 6-10 were closer to the habitual diet, so part of the shift may be a return to the usual community
  • Gene potential and relative abundance, not gene expression, butyrate or cell numbers
  • MAGs hold -% of the DNA; unassembled genomes, upstream genes and the amino-acid routes are not seen

Our take on AI

Our take on AI: fast, but guide it step by step

  • Advantage: it is very fast and can analyse a huge amount of data
  • Challenge: it is difficult to ask good questions about data you did not analyse yourself
  • Advice: guide the AI step by step, so you can control the output better

Take-home

Butyrate gene potential was maintained but redistributed

In this gut, but fell and ptb and buk rose from about day , while bcd stayed level.

  • A few genomes carry the change: Agathobacter and Roseburia fall, Acetatifactor rises
  • The whole community shifts at the same time
  • One person, ten days, diet confounded with time: no causal claim
