How does the relative abundance of B. angulatum change when the diet goes from high to low carbohydrate?

The group's own words: Decided to look into one specific organism instead of the entire community. We knew that the sequencing would get us tons of data, so instead of trying to look at all of it, we narrowed it into one specific organism. Deciding for a specific organism was more difficult but from a very quick look in the data we identified the presence of a Bifidobacteria which was described in literature as a common gut microbiome carbohydrate degrading organism. Therefore, we decided to look into how the relative abundance of that specific microbe changed as a result of the change in diet from high carb to low carb. We based our analysis on the hypothesis that the relative abundance of Bifidobacterium angulatum would be high in the beginning of day 1 (because he was carb-loading for a week before) and would continue to be high until day 5, after which it would decrease from day 6-10 as a result of the low carb diet.

Diet: days 1-5 followed about a week of a similar high-carbohydrate diet (information from the data owner). The hypothesis is the group's own.

Lab workflow: DNA extraction, library with the Rapid Barcoding Kit (SQK-RBK114.24), all ten samples on one PromethION flow cell (R10.4.1). Reads were then basecalled, assembled together and binned into MAGs (genomes reconstructed from the mixture).

Rising criterion: at least -fold the median of the four days before and higher than the day before.

The group did not stop at the rise of B. angulatum. Questions to the AI agent: did other MAGs also rise on day 5, was day 5 unusual compared with other days, and did the rising MAGs share characteristics? Answers from the data: 9 MAGs met the criterion on day 5, but days 6-10 had 12-23 each by the same rule. For days 6-9 the four days before include high-carbohydrate days, so MAGs that respond to the diet count as risers; with each MAG first divided by its own median of its diet period, days 6-10 had 5-16 risers, and day 5 lies in the middle. The risers had no clear shared trait: 7 of 9 are Bacillota (78%), as common as among the other MAGs (74%); they carry no more degrading CAZyme genes than the others (median 15.1 against 17.1 per 1,000 genes).

Limitations

What the data cannot show

Study design

  • One individual, only 10 days
  • Carbohydrates and calories changed together
  • Variable sequencing depth
  • No replicate: one sample per day (our addition)

Research question

  • Predicted genes do not confirm enzyme activity
  • Relative abundance is not absolute abundance
  • No causal link to diet can be established
  • The species was chosen after we looked at the data (our addition)

AI collaboration

  • The AI can overinterpret results
  • Analyses depend on the questions we ask
  • Outputs need critical evaluation

Diet facts: information from the data owner. Items marked "our addition" were added by the AI from the group's workflow; the group decides whether to keep them.

The three columns are the group's own. Two additions are on the slide, marked as such; more are possible, all from the group's actual analyses:

(our addition) No replicate: each day is one sample, so the day-5 peak rests on one sample per day and one DNA extraction and library. Revision 1 (Claude) added a second library of each day's DNA as a technical replicate.

(our addition) Selection: the species was chosen after a first look at the data, from the literature on bifidobacteria as carbohydrate degraders; a pattern in a species picked this way is easier to over-read.

(our addition) Diet is confounded with time: days 1-5 followed about a week of a similar high-carbohydrate diet (information from the data owner), so the diet did not change at the start, and the second period is closer to the habitual diet of the person.

(our addition) GH13 is common: 87% of the 421 MAGs carry at least one GH13 gene, so having GH13 genes is not specific to B. angulatum.

(our addition) Day 5 is not unusual: other days had as many or more rising MAGs (slide 8).

Take-home

Take-home messages

Biology

  • B. angulatum carries many carbohydrate genes (, of them GH13), but what they mean for function is uncertain
  • Its share peaked on day 5, but one person and ten days cannot explain why

AI collaboration

  • The AI helped us explore the data and test alternatives
  • Critical thinking is essential
  • In hindsight: explore the whole community first, then choose a species by its change over time or its metabolic potential
Suggestions
  • (our suggestion) Write down what you expect before asking the AI.
  • (our suggestion) Ask for a replicate or a control before trusting a single-day peak.

The four points are the group's own, shortened. Check on the wording: B. angulatum is not the most abundant species.
