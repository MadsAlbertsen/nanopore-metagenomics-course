What functions are predicted for the genes of Bifidobacterium angulatum, and how does its relative abundance vary over 10 days of a diet change from high to low carbohydrate and calories?

The group's own words: Decided to look into one specific organism instead of the entire community. We knew that the sequencing would get us tons of data, so instead of trying to look at all of it, we narrowed it into one specific organism. Deciding for a specific organism was more difficult but from a very quick look in the data we identified the presence of a Bifidobacteria which was described in literature as a common gut microbiome carbohydrate degrading organism. Therefore, we decided to look into how the relative abundance of that specific microbe changed as a result of the change in diet from high carb to low carb. We based our analysis on the hypothesis that the relative abundance of Bifidobacterium angulatum would be high in the beginning of day 1 (because he was carb-loading for a week before) and would continue to be high until day 5, after which it would decrease from day 6-10 as a result of the low carb diet.

Diet: days 1-5 followed about a week of a similar high-carbohydrate diet (information from the data owner). The hypothesis is the group's own.

Rows: the MAGs with the largest fold rise from day 4 to day 5 (share on day 5 / share on day 4) among MAGs with a mean share of at least %; B. angulatum (-fold) shown on top, set apart from them. Rising criterion: at least -fold the median of the four days before and higher than the day before.

The group did not stop at the rise of B. angulatum. Questions to the AI agent: did other MAGs also rise on day 5, was day 5 unusual compared with other days, and did the rising MAGs share characteristics? Caveat: the choice of baseline and the two diet periods affect these comparisons. Answers from the data: 9 MAGs met the criterion on day 5, but days 6-10 had 12-23 each by the same rule. For days 6-9 the four days before include high-carbohydrate days, so MAGs that respond to the diet count as risers; with each MAG first divided by its own median of its diet period, days 6-10 had 5-16 risers, and day 5 lies in the middle. The risers had no clear shared trait: 7 of 9 are Bacillota (78%), as common as among the other MAGs (74%); they carry no more degrading CAZyme genes than the others (median 15.1 against 17.1 per 1,000 genes).

Limitations

Limitations and lessons learned

Study design

  • One individual, only 10 days
  • Carbohydrates and calories changed together
  • Variable sequencing depth
  • No replicate: one sample per day

Research question

  • Predicted genes do not confirm enzyme activity
  • Relative abundance is not absolute abundance
  • No significant link to diet has been identified
  • Our focus on one species may have limited what we discovered in the wider community

AI collaboration

  • The AI can overinterpret results
  • Analyses depend on the questions we ask
  • Outputs need critical evaluation

Study design: one person, ten days, carbohydrates and calories changed together (diet facts: information from the data owner), sequencing depth varied between samples, and there is no replicate: one sample per day.

Research question: predicted genes do not confirm enzyme activity; relative abundance is not absolute abundance; we found no significant link to diet; and by focusing on one species we may have missed things in the wider community.

AI collaboration: the AI can overinterpret results, the answers depend on the questions we ask, and outputs need critical evaluation.

Take-home

Take-home messages

Biology

  • B. angulatum carries many carbohydrate genes (, of them GH13), but its relative abundance is very low (mean % of the sequenced bases, peak % on day ), so it is unlikely to be a main contributor to carbohydrate degradation in this person's gut
  • Its share peaked on day 5, but one person and ten days cannot explain why
  • Functional genes show only potential; further analysis is needed to see the phenotype

AI collaboration

  • AI helped us explore the data and challenge our findings, but critical scientific thinking remained essential
  • In hindsight: explore the whole community first, then choose a species by its change over time or its metabolic potential

Biology: B. angulatum carries 83 carbohydrate-related genes, 9 of them GH13. Its relative abundance is very low: a mean of 0.10% of the sequenced bases over the ten days (peak 0.29% on day 5; shares of the filtered bases of each day's sample). With such a low share it is unlikely to be a main contributor to carbohydrate degradation in this person's gut. Gene predictions show potential, not activity; further analysis is needed to see the phenotype. Its share peaked on day 5, but one person and ten days cannot explain why.

AI collaboration: the AI helped us explore the data and challenge our findings, but critical scientific thinking remained essential. In hindsight: explore the whole community first, then choose a species by its change over time or its metabolic potential.
