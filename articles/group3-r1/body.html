Genome-resolved butyrate gene potential over ten days in one person: the CoA-transferase route fell, and the producer genomes compensated no more than random genome sets

Abstract

Butyrate is made by gut bacteria through a final step catalysed either by butyryl-CoA:acetate CoA-transferase (but) or by phosphate butyryltransferase and butyrate kinase (ptb, buk). Redundancy among butyrate producers is thought to stabilise this function, but this has rarely been tested at the level of genomes over short time scales. We followed one person over ten daily stool samples, with a change from a high-carbohydrate, high-calorie to a low-carbohydrate, low-calorie vegetarian diet after day 5 (information from the participant; a similar high-calorie diet had run for about a week before day 1, and the second diet was closer to the habitual one). Nanopore metagenomes were co-assembled and binned into genomes (MAGs). Gene potential was measured as copies per 1,000 genes of the binned community, each MAG weighted by its share of the day's bases; the kinase route was counted only where ptb and buk are neighbours. The CoA-transferase gene fell (log2 ); among all 126 ways of splitting the ten days into two sets of five, the diet split gave the largest change (rank ), and the fall held after removing any single genome. The kinase route rose (log2 ) but halved without one genome, (log2 ). The genomes with a complete route held -% of the bases, yet their synchrony lay at the th percentile of random genome sets matched in size and abundance: no compensation beyond chance. These are relative gene potentials in one person, with diet confounded with time.

Keywords: butyrate; functional redundancy; long-read metagenomics; metagenome-assembled genomes; time series; diet

Introduction

Butyrate is the main energy source of colonic epithelial cells and is linked to anti-inflammatory effects in the gut [1]. Most butyrate producers in the human colon are Bacillota that use the acetyl-CoA pathway, in which butyryl-CoA dehydrogenase (bcd, with its electron-transfer flavoprotein partners) forms butyryl-CoA [2, 3]. The final step then takes one of two routes. In the CoA-transferase route, butyryl-CoA:acetate CoA-transferase (but) passes the CoA to acetate; in the kinase route, phosphate butyryltransferase (ptb) and butyrate kinase (buk) work in sequence and yield ATP [2]. In colonic isolates the kinase route is rare and its two genes sit together [4], while but is common and has served as a marker gene for the producer group [5]. A survey of 2,387 metagenomes and metatranscriptomes found but to be the main terminal enzyme in adults [6].

The same survey proposed, from longitudinal data, that the diversity of butyrate producers supports functional stability during ordinary disturbances [6]. This is one instance of a wider idea: many taxa can carry the same function, so the function stays steady while the taxa that carry it change [7]. Functional redundancy has been called central to the stability of the human gut microbiome, yet it has seldom been tested quantitatively [8, 9]. In vitro, short-chain fatty acid output was reproducible across donors with different communities [10]. In vivo tests have mostly used gene totals from short-read gene catalogues. A gene total cannot tell whether the same genomes stayed or different genomes replaced each other. It also counts homologues outside a butyrate route, such as buk without a neighbouring ptb.

Dense sampling shows that the human gut community can change within days [11, 12], and diets low in carbohydrate have lowered the Roseburia / Eubacterium rectale group and faecal butyrate [13-15]. In ecology, compensation among members of a group is measured by their synchrony: if members rise and fall out of step, their total varies less than they do [16]. Applied to the butyrate producers of one gut, this asks whether the producer genomes compensated for each other's changes. Relative abundance data impose a fixed total, which creates negative covariance by itself [17, 18], so any such test needs a null model built from the same data.

Long-read metagenomics assembles long contigs and often complete genomes from stool [19, 20]. Gene neighbourhoods on one contig can then separate a ptb-buk pair from a lone buk, and every gene copy is attributed to one genome. The group's submitted article used ten daily long-read metagenomes of one person, around a change in diet, to track the two final steps. It found a fall of but and a rise of ptb and buk. The reviewers accepted the fall of but but not the rise, which depended on buk outside the route and on one genome.

Aim. We asked three questions of the same data: (1) with one primary measure and the kinase route defined by gene neighbourhood, how did the two final steps change, and on which days; (2) how much of each change rests on single genomes; and (3) did the genomes with a complete route compensate for each other, keeping their total steadier than random genome sets of the same size and abundance? The design is one person and ten days, so the results describe this gut and do not separate diet from time.

Materials and methods

Participant, sampling and diet. One adult provided one stool sample per day for ten consecutive days. As reported by the participant, days 1-5 followed a high-carbohydrate, high-calorie vegetarian diet and days 6-10 a low-carbohydrate, low-calorie vegetarian diet; the second diet was closer to the participant's habitual diet, and a similar high-calorie diet had been eaten for about a week before day 1. Food intake was not recorded, so carbohydrate, calories, protein and fat cannot be separated. The participant agreed to the use of the data for teaching and to publication of derived data; for a journal submission, approval by an ethics committee and written informed consent would have to be documented here.

DNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing. The DNA extraction method is not recorded in the project notes and is therefore not stated. Libraries were made with the Rapid Barcoding Kit (SQK-RBK114-24), one barcode per day, and sequenced on one PromethION flow cell (R10.4.1). Reads were basecalled with Dorado 2.1.2 (super-accuracy model v5.2.0, with 6mA and 4mC/5mC modification calling) and demultiplexed with Dorado's default barcode trimming. There were no technical replicates.

Read processing. Reads with a mean quality below Q10 or shorter than 1,000 bp were removed (chopper), and adapters were trimmed and reads with internal adapters split with Porechop_ABI. The kept reads per sample are in Table 1: million reads and Gbp in all, - Gbp per sample, read N50 - kb and mean read quality Q-Q.

Assembly, binning and MAG quality. The reads of all ten days were co-assembled with mmlong2 v1.2.1 [20] (assembler myloasm 0.2.0, extended binning mode, the ten samples as separate coverage profiles). The final assembly had contigs ( Gbp, contig N50 kb, flagged circular). MAG completeness and contamination were estimated by CheckM2 [21] within mmlong2; high quality (HQ) means more than 90% complete, less than 5% contaminated, with the rRNA genes and at least 18 tRNAs, and the remaining MAGs are medium quality (MQ). This gave MAGs, of them HQ. Taxonomy was assigned with GTDB-Tk v2.5.2 and GTDB release 226 [23]. Contigs were screened for methylation motifs that do not fit their MAG (Nanomotif motifs, then the project's own motif-based screen).

Gene calls. Genes were predicted with Pyrodigal 3.7.1 [25, 26] in metagenome mode; these calls share every stop codon with Bakta v1.11.4 (database 6.0 light) [22]. bcd (K00248), ptb (K00634), buk (K00929), atoD (K01034) and atoA (K01035) were found with their KOfam profiles and thresholds [24] using PyHMMER 0.12.3 [27]. but has no clean KOfam profile and was taken as the genes Bakta names exactly "butyryl-CoA:acetate CoA-transferase" ( genes, 63-100% identical to the characterised But enzymes [28]); genes with the broader name "acetyl-CoA hydrolase/transferase" are only -% identical and are not counted. A ptb and a buk are a pair when they lie within three genes of each other on one contig. Each call was graded as confident, borderline or doubtful from its score relative to the threshold, its expected neighbour (Etf for bcd, the partner gene for ptb, buk, atoD and atoA), the methylation screen of its contig, the MAG's contamination and, for but, identity to the references (rules in the legend of Table 2); the rules were fixed after the KOfam scores had been seen but before the classes were computed.

Abundance and the primary measure. A MAG's share on day d is the percent of that day's sequenced bases on its contigs, each contig counting its length times its mean read depth (minimap2 mapping within mmlong2):

a(m,d) = 100 × Σcontigs of m length × depth(d) / bases sequenced on day d

Gene potential is copies per 1,000 genes of the binned community, each MAG weighted by its share:

G(g,d) = 1000 × Σm a(m,d) × copies(g,m) / Σm a(m,d) × genes(m)

The kinase route is counted as the number of ptb-buk pairs (one copy per route, as for but), and the ratio of the two final steps on day d is R(d) = G(but,d) / G(pair,d). A change between periods is log2 of the mean of days 6-10 over the mean of days 1-5. The share of bases is not a share of cells: a large genome contributes more bases per cell. All values are relative; no total microbial load was measured.

Statistics. The analyses are descriptive. For each daily series we ranked the diet split (days 1-5 against 6-10) among all 126 different ways to split ten days into two sets of five, by the size of the difference in mean log2 value (for the community, by the PERMANOVA pseudo-F on Bray-Curtis dissimilarity); a rank of 1 means no other split separates the days more. We also give the first day of days 6-10 outside the range of days 1-5, and the best single breakpoint in time. Leave-one-genome-out repeats each period change without each gene-carrying MAG in turn. Synchrony of the route guild (MAGs with bcd plus but or a ptb-buk pair) was measured on the MAG shares as φ = var(guild total) / (Σ standard deviations of the members)² [16], with 1 for members moving in step and 0 for complete compensation, together with the variance ratio (variance of the total over the summed member variances) and the coefficient of variation (CV) of the total. The null distribution came from random sets of non-guild MAGs, matched to the guild member by member (each replaced by one of the unused non-guild MAGs closest in mean share), and from unmatched random sets of the same size. The method recovers its known answers (φ = 1 for identical members, 0 for mirror-image members, about 1/n for independent members). To check the role of sequencing depth, the reads of each sample were thinned at random (binomially, per contig) to the bases of the smallest sample ( Gbp), times, and all measures recomputed; this models sampling noise only, not how depth affects assembly or binning. Code: Python 3 with pandas and NumPy (np26 scripts/43_project_group3.py and 43c_group3_revision1.py, with the package np26).

The summed share of the route MAGs varied between % and % of the bases (CV %), while its members varied far more (share-weighted mean CV %; Figure 5A). The but genomes and the kinase genomes moved in opposite directions (Pearson r over the ten days). Their synchrony was low (φ = ; variance ratio ), which looks like compensation. However, random sets of non-guild MAGs matched in size and abundance were just as asynchronous: the guild's φ lay at the th percentile of the matched null (median ; 90% range -; Figure 5B), its variance ratio at the th and the CV of its total at the th. Unmatched random sets gave the th percentile. The but genomes alone (φ , th percentile of their own matched null) and the kinase genomes alone (φ , th percentile) were not unusual either. The guild total did not separate the diet periods (rank of 126). The steadiness of the total is therefore what any large set of genomes shows in these relative data, and gives no evidence of compensation specific to butyrate producers.

Discussion

In ten daily metagenomes of one person, the CoA-transferase gene but fell after the diet change, and the change is the most robust result in these data. The diet split was the most extreme of all 126 splits for but, the fall held without any single genome, without doubtful calls, on HQ MAGs only and at equal sequencing depth, and it was carried by known CoA-transferase producers such as Agathobacter and Roseburia [5]. The kinase route rose, but this is a weaker result. ptb is called near its score threshold, the rise began before the switch, and it halved without , a genus first described from mice [29]. Restricting buk to genomes where it lies next to ptb, as the reviewers asked, did not change the direction. buk without ptb rose as much as the pairs, in Bacteroidaceae that have no ptb next to buk.

The new question was whether the producer genomes compensated for each other. The total share of the route genomes was steady and its two parts moved in opposite directions, which on its own looks like functional redundancy at work [6, 7]. The null model says otherwise. Random sets of genomes of the same number and abundance, drawn from the rest of the community, were just as asynchronous, and the guild sat near the middle of that distribution. Part of the apparent compensation is arithmetic: shares of a fixed total must offset each other [17, 18]. The rest is what a large group of genomes gives anyway, since independent members already average out. Redundancy may still stabilise butyrate formation in this gut, but these data give no evidence that the producer genomes did more of it than any comparable set of genomes.

This matters for how redundancy is read from metagenomes. Gene totals of a functional group are steady almost by construction when the group is large, so a steady total is weak evidence for compensation [8, 9]. A genome-resolved series makes a stronger test possible, because members can be followed one by one and compared with a null drawn from the same community. The same approach would apply to larger designs, where it could separate compensation from the arithmetic of relative data.

Limitations. This is one person, one sample per day, ten days, one diet change and no technical replicates, so day-to-day noise is unknown and diet is confounded with time, with calories, and with a return towards the habitual diet after a week of the first diet. Several series moved before the switch, so a response to diet cannot be separated from drift. All values are gene potential in relative units: shares of sequenced bases, not cell counts, and not expression or butyrate. Without a measure of total microbial load [18] the direction of any absolute change is unknown, and a fall in share can arise from growth elsewhere as well as from loss. MAGs hold about four-fifths of the bases, and genomes too rare to assemble are not seen. The gene evidence is uneven: bcd is an upper bound, but is defined by a name, ptb sits at its threshold, and the upstream genes and the lysine, glutarate and 4-aminobutyrate routes were not searched [2]. Comparisons with earlier diet studies [13-15] are in direction only, since those measured taxa by other methods, in groups of people, over weeks.

Conclusions

In this ten-day series from one gut, the CoA-transferase gene of butyrate formation fell while the kinase route rose, and the kinase result rests on a single genome. Defining the kinase route by the neighbourhood of ptb and buk removes most buk calls from it without changing the direction. The genomes carrying the route kept a steady total, but no steadier than random genome sets of the same size and abundance, so this series gives no evidence of compensation specific to butyrate producers. Genome-resolved long reads plus a null model drawn from the same community make such a test possible; separating diet from time would need several people and a crossover design with a washout, replicate extractions and a measure of total load or faecal butyrate.

Data availability

The sequencing reads are not public: they come from human stool, and the reads are withheld to protect the donor's privacy. For a journal submission the route would be controlled-access deposition of the reads after removal of human sequence, for example in the European Genome-phenome Archive, with the MAGs deposited openly. The derived tables behind every figure and table of this revision are in this page's data file, and the analysis code is part of the course material (np26 scripts/43_project_group3.py and 43c_group3_revision1.py); for a submission the code would be archived with a versioned DOI.

The editorial decision was to reject with encouragement to resubmit. This revision answers the five essential points and the major points of the three reviews; the design limits that the editor named cannot be fixed by reanalysis and are stated as limits instead.
